## Current user direction

Playground belongs only to an individual project and its authenticated role. Remove the client-board entry point and reject new workspace-only scopes in the backend. Existing saved content must be preserved; two workspace boards and two project boards with 32 total items exist at the initial inspection. The user has been asked whether to archive the former scopes or transfer them to explicitly chosen projects. Until a destination is supplied, preserve legacy records without inventing a project assignment.

Opening Playground reveals a board rising from the bottom over the entire viewport, including the sidebar and client/project headers. Closing slides it back down before unmounting. Its fullscreen native dialog uses the browser's top layer so canvas clipping and shell stacking cannot restrict it. Covered app controls are inert and body scrolling is locked until close. Escape and native cancellation route through the existing unsaved/busy protections. Respect reduced motion and preserve the project's viewport and state beneath the layer. This later user request supersedes the original nonmodal, project-contained presentation.

The existing upload entry remains useful: opening Playground temporarily closes the upload dialog without unmounting its form or losing selected files/text; the project-owned layer opens, then returns to that same form after its exit animation. Agency, client and designer project scopes and private files remain isolated.

After this interaction is implemented and verified, improve the video feature's loading and processing efficiency. Measure current behavior before choosing changes, preserve metadata sanitization, immutable publication and timed feedback, and verify any claimed improvement using repeatable measurements. Read-only investigation may occur during Playground implementation; video changes start after its acceptance checks pass.
